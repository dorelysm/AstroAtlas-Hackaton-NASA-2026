# Processes, not just places: three case studies (15 Nov 2026)

The challenge asks to think about processes (volcanism, erosion, ice dynamics) and not only places. Our method compares places, so **processes are treated as qualitative case studies**, tied to what we do measure and to sources. This is context, **not a computed or validated result**. The sources are search results read in this session; the full articles were not opened.

## 1. Basaltic volcanism and lava tubes
**Process:** lava flows through channels that roof over and leave tubes; when the roof falls in, a skylight remains.
- **Moon:** 278 catalogued pits (LROC); the one in Marius Hills is the first confirmed as a probable tube skylight, and a 2024 radar study points to an accessible conduit under the Mare Tranquillitatis pit ([Universe Today on LRO](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/lro-takes-closer-look-at-moon-caves); [LROC Pits Atlas](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/atlases/pits/2)). A study with GRAIL gravity proposes more candidates.
- **Mars:** 1,062 cave-entrance candidates (USGS), concentrated in Tharsis ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/center-news/caves-mars)).
- **Earth:** the Pyroduct catalogue documents 93 tubes; **8 are in Harrat Al-Shaam (Jordan)** ([caves-and-lava-tubes.md](caves-and-lava-tubes.md)). Haruj (Libya) is a field of some 45,000 km² with about 150 volcanic centres and very well preserved forms ([NASA Earth Observatory](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/haruj-volcanic-field-87939/)), but it is not in the tube catalogue.
- **Ours:** the Mars candidates (Harrat ash-Shamah and Haruj) are basaltic fields; geology 0.85 to 0.86.
- **Novelty (preregistered check, [../preregistrations/novelty.md](../preregistrations/novelty.md)):** **Harrat ash-Shamah has been used as a planetary-pit analog**: the "jubot" depressions in the north-west of the field were presented in 2018 and 2019 abstracts ([LPSC 2019](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019LPI....50.3187N/abstract)); no study of the field as a geological analog turned up. **Haruj has no study of its own as an analog in this search**, although other basaltic fields (Mauna Ulu, Pinacate, San Francisco, Wudalianchi) and another Libyan caldera, Wau-an-Namus, have been used for Mars' aeolian ridges. "No evidence" does not prove that no literature exists.
- **What we do not know:** whether the Harrat tubes are inside our cell; the model does not see the tubes.
- **For exploration:** testing cave sounding (radar, gravimetry) and skylight-entry robots in real tubes, which is what is needed before exploring the Marius Hills or Tranquillitatis pits.

## 2. Aeolian transport: dust, dunes and wind erosion
**Process:** wind moves sand and dust, forms dunes and ventifacts, and raises dust devils.
- **Mars:** dust and storms are an operational factor; Martian dunes are mostly dark mafic sand, unlike terrestrial dunes, which are quartz-rich ([Europlanet](https://europlanet.dlr.de/Mars/mars_terres_ana_morph_aeolian.html)).
- **Earth:** Sahara dust-devil tracks have been proposed as an analog of those on Mars, although they are smaller (2 to 20 m wide versus 10 to 200 m) ([2008 abstract](https://scisoc.confex.com/crops/2008am/webprogram/Paper47752.html)). The dunes at Gale (the Namib dune on Mars) have been compared with terrestrial dunes such as Maspalomas ([Cornwall et al. 2018](https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/ws/files/12423600/Cornwall_et_al_Icarus_article_2018.pdf)).
- **Ours (context only, MERRA-2 2019):** Haruj has 187 µg/m³ of surface dust and Harrat ash-Shamah 88, against less than 1 µg/m³ in the Antarctic cells. A basaltic field in a dusty desert would produce **dark basaltic sand**, closer to the Martian one than that of quartz dunes (this is our inference, not measured).
- **What we do not know:** there is no comparable counterpart on Mars within the model (the dust opacity from MCS/TES would be needed), so it is not scored. We have measured nothing about sand or dunes.
- **For exploration:** testing the effect of dust on solar panels, seals and sensors, and dune drift under rovers.

## 3. Subsurface ice and permafrost
**Process:** ice is preserved under a dry soil layer and permafrost contracts and cracks.
- **Mars:** the shallow ice of the north (Phoenix, Arcadia) is a potential resource; at higher obliquity ice is globally stable (review cited in [Core](https://core.ac.uk/works/24780217)).
- **Earth:** the Antarctic Dry Valleys are considered the best Earth analog of Mars subsurface ice, with dry permafrost over ice-cemented soil ([NASA NTRS 20150023012](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150023012)). One source warns that **no part of Earth reproduces the temperature swings of a single Martian day**.
- **Ours:** it is the **weakest** aspect. Our ice variable (snow) fails precisely at the Dry Valleys (−0.75), and we have no measure of ground ice. The atlas lists deserts without snow (Gobi, Death Valley, Atacama), which are dry but have no ice. (Later work with Odyssey neutrons and a real permafrost map improved the cold case; see [../preregistrations/polar-ice.md](../preregistrations/polar-ice.md) and [../preregistrations/permafrost-map.md](../preregistrations/permafrost-map.md).)
- **For exploration:** testing drills and ice sensors in the Dry Valleys before a mission to the northern plains of Mars. It is a use that does not need our model: it is already in the literature.

## What the three cases show
Only the first (basaltic volcanism) connects our results with a concrete process and a hint of novelty. In the second we offer context (dust) without a counterpart, and in the third the already-known analog is better than anything our model produces. It is a useful conclusion: **the model is stronger for volcanic geology than for ice or surface processes**, and that is how it has to be presented.
