# Preregistration: novelty check of the candidates (15 Nov 2026, written BEFORE searching)

Question: the challenge values little-studied places as analogs. Have the candidates (Harrat ash-Shamah and Haruj) already been proposed or used as an analog of Mars or the Moon in the open literature?

## Method
Standard web search with the queries fixed here (the search engine is US-only and does not access Google Scholar or paid databases).

| Group | Queries |
|---|---|
| Candidate A, Harrat ash-Shamah | (1) `Harrat ash-Shamah Mars analog` (2) `Harrat Ash Shaam basalt planetary analogue Moon Mars Jordan` (3) `Jordan basalt plateau Azraq Safawi planetary analog Mars` |
| Candidate B, Haruj | (4) `Haruj Libya planetary analogue Mars` (5) `Al Haruj al Aswad volcanic field Mars Moon analog` (6) `Libya Haruj basalt lava field analog for Mars or Moon` |
| Positive control (must come out "studied") | (7) `Pinacate volcanic field Mars analog` (8) `Craters of the Moon lunar astronaut training analog` |
| Comparison control (unselected basaltic field) | (9) `Harrat Khaybar Mars analog` |

## Classification criterion (per region)
- **Studied as an analog:** at least one result (article, conference abstract, agency page) whose title or text **explicitly** links that region with Mars or the Moon as an analog.
- **Mentioned in passing:** it appears in a result about planetary science or comparative volcanism without being the object of study.
- **No evidence in this search:** no result meets the above.

## Validity of the method
If the positive control (7 and 8) does **not** come out as "studied", the search is not useful to measure novelty and the result is discarded. A "no evidence" result **does not prove** that no literature exists: only that this search did not find it.
No queries are added after seeing the results; if another search were made, it is reported separately.

## Results (preregistration hash `5360e7bf0a750427`; the 9 queries were made exactly as written, without adding any)

| Region | Classification | What was found |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive control: Pinacate** | Studied as an analog | PSI (Ives flow, 13,000 years, NASA Earth-Mars project), a 1974 article comparing it with Mars, NASA and University of Arizona pages: [PSI](https://www.psi.edu/blog/investigations-of-lava-flows-in-the-pinacate-volcanic-field-as-analogues-for-mars) |
| **Positive control: Craters of the Moon** | Studied as an analog | Apollo astronaut training and NASA BASALT field site; its rock is chemically similar to rocks at Gusev (Mars) |
| **Candidate A: Harrat ash-Shamah** | **Studied as an analog, partially** | The "jubot" depressions in the north-west of the field were presented as a **planetary-pit analog** with aerial and terrestrial LiDAR: [LPSC 2019 abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019LPI....50.3187N/abstract) and [AGU 2018 abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018AGUFM.P31H3791N). They are conference abstracts: no peer-reviewed article was found in these results. No study of the lava field as a geological analog of Mars turned up. The Wadi Rum simulations (PETRA) are habitat, not geological |
| **Candidate B: Haruj** | **No evidence in this search** | Only descriptions of the field (NASA, USGS, Live Science) and a travel page that calls it "lunar" (an impression, not an assessment). A 2019 study uses **another** Libyan caldera, Wau-an-Namus, as an analog of Mars' transverse aeolian ridges |
| Comparison: Harrat Khaybar (Saudi Arabia) | Mentioned in passing | It resembles Mars visually and a 2026 review points to the potential of Saudi Arabia as an analog, without naming Khaybar |

**Validity:** the positive control came out well, so the search is useful for detecting analog literature when it exists.

## What changes in our conclusions
- **Correction:** in `context/processes.md` it was said that there was no analog study for Harrat ash-Shamah. **That was incomplete**: there are pit studies (2018 and 2019 abstracts). It was corrected.
- **A is not a "new" candidate in the strict sense**, but its use as an analog of **pits and skylights** fits the volcanic use case and the 8 Pyroduct tubes.
- **B (Haruj) is the least-studied candidate as an analog** according to this search. But its 5° cell was badly centred and its topographic similarity was low (0.32), and access to Libya is complicated.

## Limits
A US web search without Google Scholar or paid databases; the queries are in English (there may be literature in Arabic, French or Italian that does not appear). **"No evidence" does not prove absence of literature.** The results are search-engine summaries; the articles were not read.
